## A. Self-hosted browser

1. The organizer signs in to Meeting Hub, pastes the URL or code, selects the verified occurrence and confirms the recording purpose. After the authorization check below, **Send bot** starts an isolated Linux container. Playwright opens Chrome with camera and microphone off, a virtual display (Xvfb for headed Chrome on a server), and a private PulseAudio output. The guest enters the name **Meeting Hub Recorder** and selects **Ask to join** or **Join now** as offered. [Google guest join flow](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/9303069?hl=en) · [Playwright on Linux](https://playwright.dev/docs/ci) · [FFmpeg audio input devices](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-devices.html#pulse)
2. The organizer opens Meet and admits that name if prompted. A guest can join without a Google account where policy permits. For an uninvited guest at a personal-account meeting, Google assigns admission to the creator. Workspace admission depends on host management, invitations, Open/Trusted/Restricted access, whether knocking is allowed and any separate waiting room. Disabling knocking can reject an uninvited bot before anyone sees a request. A sign-in-only room needs an allowed Google identity for the bot, potentially an invitation to that exact identity and host admission. Google can block sign-in from software-controlled browsers. The requester’s Hub login does not sign the bot in to Google. [Personal and Workspace access](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/9302870?co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop&hl=en) · [Knocking controls](https://support.google.com/a/users/answer/11989526?hl=en) · [Waiting rooms](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/16523457) · [Google sign-in restrictions](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/7675428?hl=en)
3. The bot posts the notice below. The organizer obtains agreement and presses **Start recording** in Hub. Only then does a capture-specific FFmpeg process read the private output’s monitor inside this container. Write one timestamped audio original, e.g. lossless PCM in WAV, to bounded encrypted scratch space. This captures the decoded call mix, including overlapping voices and any audible presentation. Packet loss, a quiet microphone or a shared room mic affects the original. The virtual output provides no isolated speaker tracks. Phase 5 diarizes the mix and allows human correction. [FFmpeg PulseAudio input](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-devices.html#pulse) · [ADR 0005](../adr/0005-transcription-and-speakers.md)
4. The host ends the call, or the last human leaves. Our proposed worker watches the end/removal screen and roster, with a short alone grace period. The host leaving alone does not establish that the meeting ended. Distinguish a disconnected browser, missing controls or a frozen renderer from a clean end. Flush the audio, close the browser and import the original. The organizer watches processing on the meeting page, then opens **Summary** and follows a citation into the transcript. [Google end-for-everyone controls](https://support.google.com/a/users/answer/11989526?hl=en) · [ADR 0006](../adr/0006-cited-meeting-documents.md)

**Operations and terms.** Budget one sandboxed, non-root browser, private audio sink, encoder and scratch volume per concurrent meeting. Monitor browser/encoder liveness, elapsed time, output bytes, packet gaps and audio level. Silence alone cannot distinguish a quiet room from a broken sink. Persist finalized segments if recovery is wanted. A crash cannot replay missed speech. If UI changes break admission or capture, disable new dispatches and test the revised flow in a controlled room. Treat CAPTCHA, account challenges, policy blocks, denials and removal as terminal for that attempt. The frequency and a universal Google knock timeout are **unverified**. Google’s terms address interference, bypassing protections and automated access contrary to machine-readable instructions. Meet’s policy covers unwanted automated content and evasion. We found no published Google approval for running this commercial browser recorder. Review the applicable account and Workspace contracts before testing beyond our own rooms. [Playwright container isolation](https://playwright.dev/docs/docker) · [Google Terms](https://policies.google.com/terms) · [Meet acceptable use](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/9847091) · [Workspace acceptable use](https://workspace.google.com/terms/use_policy/)

## B. Google Meet Media API

**For a new Meeting Hub customer, the flow ends at paste.** Show that live capture through this method is unavailable and offer the existing upload workflow. Google admitted the API to Developer Preview in February 2025 and discontinued it in October 2026. Only previously approved credentials and staging environments have the transition window. Program terms restrict pre-GA public apps and external testing. The supported Meet REST API can retrieve artifacts a meeting actually generated. Native recording depends on edition, admin settings and host privileges. REST artifact retrieval cannot deliver a recorder to an arbitrary Gmail call. [Meet release notes](https://developers.google.com/workspace/meet/release-notes) · [Preview program terms](https://developers.google.com/workspace/preview) · [REST artifacts](https://developers.google.com/workspace/meet/api/guides/artifacts) · [Native recording eligibility](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/9308681)

For an **already enrolled test group**, the historical path was as follows. Google has withdrawn several public guide URLs. The historical details below were checked against indexed copies of Google’s own guides. Its admin help, program notice and reference-client source remain available.

1. The requester pastes the code and authorizes the app as a particular Google user. The preview required the Cloud project, OAuth principal **and every participant** to be enrolled. It required the Meet REST API to be enabled, a restricted media scope (`meetings.conference.media.audio.readonly` for audio) and a meeting-space read scope. Google documented restricted-scope verification and a security assessment for server storage or transmission. General DPP enrollment used a Workspace account and a registered project. There was no need for an independent Google account to impersonate a guest bot. Ordinary personal Gmail or unenrolled external attendees could not be promised coverage. [Historical Google prerequisites and scopes](https://developers.google.com/workspace/meet/media-api/guides/get-started) · [Program enrollment](https://developers.google.com/workspace/preview) · [Google restricted-scope policy](https://developers.google.com/workspace/workspace-api-user-data-developer-policy)
2. The host permits the app in the meeting’s Calendar settings. The organizer posts the notice and obtains the Hub consent receipt before requesting WebRTC media, because a successful connection can deliver audio immediately. The waiting state is for app admission/consent. Workspace admin settings control access and whether someone from the host’s organization or only a host/co-host can approve. At least one person in the call had to have previously accepted the terms. In the documented consumer-hosted case the initiator had to be present to consent. Meet informed participants of active access and any participant could stop it. Enrollment, OAuth approval and meeting consent were three separate conditions. The preview also excluded meetings with underage accounts, watermarks or extra meeting encryption, and excluded some clients such as third-party room hardware. Google also specified minimum mobile app versions. A universal consent-prompt timeout is **unverified**. [Historical Google conference lifecycle](https://developers.google.com/workspace/meet/media-api/guides/overview) · [Historical client restrictions](https://developers.google.com/workspace/meet/media-api/guides/get-started) · [Workspace admin consent settings](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/meet/control-media-api-access-in-google-meet)
3. After the Hub consent gate, the receive-only client takes audio over WebRTC. Google’s client negotiated exactly three receive-only audio streams and up to three video streams. Meet could switch their sources to the three loudest speakers. RTP contributing-source IDs map frames to participants. This permits speaker-associated audio for delivered frames, with no guarantee of every voice during four-way overlap. Mix by timestamp for Phase 4 and preserve gaps. The organizer can also stop access in Meet. [Google reference client](https://github.com/googleworkspace/meet-media-api-samples/blob/main/cpp/api/media_api_client_interface.h) · [Historical virtual-stream guide](https://developers.google.com/workspace/meet/media-api/guides/virtual-streams)
4. On conference end, finalize one file, import it and open the resulting Hub document. Session control distinguishes conference ended, user stopped and unhealthy transport. Honor a user stop immediately. On transport failure, flag the gap and check whether the conference is still active and consent is still valid before considering a reconnect. Denial, revoked OAuth, admin disablement or an ineligible attendee prevents capture. There is no browser knock or automated sign-in CAPTCHA loop on this path. The Google APIs terms and Workspace disclosure, limited-use, transfer and consent rules apply to the audio and derived data. No production per-hour API tariff or concurrent-media entitlement was verified. The published Meet REST request quotas measure API calls per minute and cannot size a media fleet. [Google session control](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/googleworkspace/meet-media-api-samples/main/cpp/api/session_control_resource.h) · [REST request quotas](https://developers.google.com/workspace/meet/api/guides/limits) · [Google APIs terms](https://developers.google.com/terms) · [Workspace developer policy](https://developers.google.com/workspace/workspace-api-user-data-developer-policy)

## C. Recall.ai Meeting Bot API

1. The verified organizer pastes the link/code, confirms the purpose and clicks **Send bot**. Our backend sends the canonical Meet URL and an opaque job correlation ID to Create Bot in the chosen Recall region. Set the name to **Meeting Hub Recorder** and explicitly send `recording_config: null`. Recall documents that this joins without capturing recording media, including participant/chat event streams. Bot lifecycle webhooks still work. [Create Bot](https://docs.recall.ai/reference/bot_create) · [Manual recording control](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/recording-control)
2. The organizer watches **Joining** or **Waiting for admission** and admits the bot in Meet. Recall normally joins as an anonymous guest. Gmail creator admission and Workspace access controls from A apply. If sign-in is required, the proposed guest pilot shows the reason and ends the attempt. For a later signed-in mode, Recall requires a **dedicated paid Google Workspace**, a standard non-admin bot account and Legacy SSO configuration. Recall holds signing material. The Google account display name overrides `bot_name`. Invite that account when policy requires an invite. The host’s separate waiting room, external-domain restrictions and bot blocks still apply. No sign-in success rate or CAPTCHA frequency for our tenants was verified. [Recall Meet overview](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/google-meet) · [Signed-in bot requirements](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/google-meet-login-getting-started) · [Google Meet failure codes](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/sub-codes)
3. The bot announces its purpose in chat. After everyone agrees, the organizer confirms in Hub and selects **Start recording**. Call Start Recording once with `audio_mixed_mp3: {}`, `video_mixed_mp4: null`, explicit timed retention, and unneeded participant events/meeting metadata disabled. Omit vendor transcription. Confirm the provider reports `bot.in_call_recording`. Mixed MP3 contains received meeting audio. Optional separate MP3s cover up to **16 concurrent participant audio streams** on Meet, selected from the most recent speakers. A shared mic or Meet adaptive audio can still combine people under one identity. Actual intelligibility, gaps and separability need listening tests. [Start Recording defaults](https://docs.recall.ai/reference/bot_start_recording_create) · [Mixed MP3](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/how-to-get-mixed-audio-async) · [Separate audio limits](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/how-to-get-separate-audio-per-participant-async) · [Adaptive audio caveat](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/google-meet-faq)
4. A host end, an empty room after a grace period, a removal or Hub’s stop action ends capture. Inspect `bot.call_ended` and its subcode. Explicit kicks stop further joins. Recall notes that Google’s **End meeting for everyone** can also appear as `bot_received_leave_call`, so correlate it with our stop record. When the end is ambiguous, hold the audio for organizer confirmation within the retention window before importing. For a normal end, wait for `audio_mixed.done` or `recording.done`, verify the actual audio object is ready, import it and delete the vendor media. Hub displays processing and then the cited document. Poll the regional API if webhooks are missing. [Bot events](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/bot-status-change-events) · [Recording events](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/recording-webhooks) · [Meet end behavior](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/google-meet-faq)

**Admission timers and capacity.** Recall advertises a configurable 20-minute waiting-room default. Its automatic-leave guide lists Meet’s platform wait as indefinite, while its subcode reference says 10 minutes. Google’s waiting-room help gives no universal deadline. Use our **five-minute pilot cutoff** and accept an earlier denial. Budget one vendor bot per live or waiting call, plus short-lived Hub webhook/import work. The ad-hoc pool can return HTTP 507 even when a room is valid. Check the contracted workspace limit in the dashboard and honor `Retry-After` within the join deadline. Meet livestreams and breakout rooms are outside Recall’s documented support. [Automatic leave](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/automatic-leaving-behavior) · [Subcodes](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/sub-codes) · [Google waiting rooms](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/16523457) · [Recall capacity](https://docs.recall.ai/reference/errors) · [Supported Meet modes](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/google-meet)

**Data handling and lock-in.** Recall receives the meeting URL, joins the call and processes the selected media. Choose a region before creating the bot. US West, US East, Frankfurt and Tokyo have region-local resources and credentials. Recall documents TLS in transit and encryption at rest. Set a proposed **24-hour** expiration on each manual Start Recording. Recall starts that clock when the recording reaches `done`. Delete earlier once Hub has verified its durable copy and readable audio, and reconcile the `recording.deleted` receipt. Accounts created after 12 June 2025 default to indefinite storage when retention is omitted. Vendor deletion covers recording media and its backups. Bot URLs and custom metadata have separate retention, as do downstream processors. Zero Data Retention requires a live delivery design and precludes the post-call download used here. The portable asset is the audio original. Keep Recall IDs, webhook vocabulary, SSO setup and download URLs inside the capture integration. Prices, fleet availability, Google enforcement and vendor API changes still affect dispatch. The vendor’s contract governs our use of Recall. For either browser route, a separate Calendar OAuth integration would bind us to Google’s API terms and limited-use policy for that data. Its grant covers calendar access only. Obtain its basis for operating on Meet and review customer Workspace terms. We could not verify a Google authorization covering our particular deployment. A signed-in provider bot can still be classified as risky, blocked or challenged. Treat those outcomes as final. ## Notice, consent and stopping

**Proposed pilot rule.** Only invite a known group whose organizer can control admission. Before dispatch, Hub shows who requested the recorder, what will be captured, who can access the result, the applicable capture/STT/analysis providers, retention, and how to stop or request deletion. Record the requester’s explicit authorization. In the call, obtain each participant’s affirmative agreement before starting the recorder. For the small pilot the organizer can collect replies verbally while capture is off and attest to the specific roster in Hub. Store the notice version, occurrence, confirming user, time and minimal evidence of who agreed, separately from content-free audit logs. The consent record identifies what the participants agreed to. Fully automatic start after paste would require collecting that agreement before the call and verifying everyone who can enter. [Current upload consent contract](../adr/0003-meetings-and-uploads.md) · [Google Workspace user data policy](https://developers.google.com/workspace/workspace-api-user-data-developer-policy)

Use **Meeting Hub Recorder** in the roster throughout A and C. A posts in Meet chat, C can send to everyone, and B uses Meet’s native active-app notice plus an organizer-posted message. Announce before capture and again on start. A pre-start message could read “Meeting Hub Recorder is here at the organizer’s request. With everyone’s agreement, it will record audio for a transcript and cited notes in the organizer’s workspace. Tell the organizer if you decline or want it stopped.” The host reads it aloud for people who cannot see chat. Google documents native recording notifications and optional explicit consent for its own features. A/C need their own notice. Recall can repeat chat on joins or pin a notice when supported. Pinning requires continuous chat to be off and posting can be restricted. Verify delivery with a second participant. If notice or agreement cannot be established, leave with recording off. [Native recording notices](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/9308681) · [Google explicit-consent controls](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/meet/manage-participant-consent) · [Meet chat visibility](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/9308979) · [Recall chat support](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/sending-chat-messages)

**Late joiners and withdrawal.** The host briefs and obtains agreement from expected late arrivals before admission. Use a controlled roster and repeat the notice when they enter. If someone arrives unexpectedly or objects, stop immediately. A chat receipt cannot establish their agreement. The pilot’s **Stop recording and discard** button is available to the verified organizer and authorized workspace admins. Any participant can object to the host, the host can remove A/C in Meet, and any participant could turn off B directly. A closes the encoder and leaves. C sends Stop Recording and Remove Bot From Call, then verifies shutdown and deletes any resulting media. A stop/explicit kick fences off publication even if a late completion arrives. Display **Stopping** until confirmed. Offer the ordinary deletion/purge flow for material already imported. ## Dispatch authorization and credential boundaries

- **Validate without fetching the paste.** Parse locally. Accept a bare Meet code or an HTTPS URL with the exact ASCII host `meet.google.com` and one code path component. Google documents three hyphenated alphabetic groups and a maximum of 128 characters. Normalize ASCII case. As a convenience, format ten bare letters as 3-4-3. Rebuild the URL from the parsed code and the fixed origin. Discard common Google query/fragment decoration. Reject credentials, nondefault ports, extra path components, encoded separators, lookalike hosts, IPs, short links, nicknames and redirect URLs. Do not navigate to, resolve or log a rejected input. [Meet space URI and code format](https://developers.google.com/workspace/meet/api/reference/rest/v2/spaces) · [Google’s case-insensitive code lookup](https://developers.google.com/workspace/meet/api/reference/rest/v2/spaces/get)
- **Prove authority before even knocking.** In the pilot, a coordinator observes the organizer create a fresh Meet in Google Calendar and binds that organizer, Hub workspace, exact occurrence and dispatch window to the room. Keep the join URL encrypted only while needed and use a keyed digest for equality checks. Only that Hub user can request this occurrence. Recheck membership, meeting-create/upload permissions, ownership and the workspace flag at dispatch and import. Viewers cannot send bots. Guesses about another workspace’s job return the usual 404. Possessing a URL, seeing a public event, pasting a calendar screenshot or sharing an email domain cannot establish authority. For self-service later, consider a delegated, read-only check against the requester’s primary Calendar, matching the occurrence, Meet conference data and organizer. A copied Meet link on a user-created event still needs independent host proof. For external invitees, require approval from a separately verified organizer for that occurrence. If proof is missing, refuse before contacting the room. [Calendar event and conference fields](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/v3/reference/events) · [Calendar authorization scopes](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/auth) · [ADR 0002](../adr/0002-auth-and-workspaces.md) · [Meeting permissions](../adr/0003-meetings-and-uploads.md)
- **Bound dispatch and credentials.** Permit one active bot per authorized occurrence. Enforce per-user, per-workspace and global concurrent/budget limits and a cooldown after failure. Audit actor, IDs, times and reason codes. A gets an isolated non-root browser profile and private audio. Restrict egress to the required Meet services, job control and storage. Deny host mounts and access to cloud metadata or unrelated internal services. Any later Google bot account is dedicated, least-privileged and operator-managed. For B, bind scoped OAuth tokens to the granting user and tenant, encrypt them, support revocation and expire grants. For C, keep regional API keys, verification secrets and any future SSO private key in a secret manager with rotation and access auditing. Restrict SSO to the dedicated bot Workspace. Keep passwords, cookies, audio, room links, signed media URLs and participant messages out of queues/logs. [Google OAuth best practices](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/resources/best-practices) · [Playwright container guidance](https://playwright.dev/docs/docker) · [Recall SSO key handling](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/google-meet-login-getting-started) · [Repo logging and storage conventions](../../CLAUDE.md)
- **Authenticate the return path.** Verify Recall’s signature over the raw body with the secret/header scheme for the configured endpoint, check timestamp freshness and deduplicate the signed delivery ID. Bind the provider region and bot/recording ID to a stored workspace job. A callback supplies a signal to re-read state through the authenticated regional API. Accept media only from that lookup through a bounded HTTPS download to approved vendor/storage origins. Validate resolved addresses and any redirect, deny private/link-local destinations and enforce time and byte limits. Pass local files only to media tools. [Recall webhook verification](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/authenticating-requests-from-recallai) · [Regional resources](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/regions) · [Repo media security](../adr/0004-processing-pipeline.md)

## Capture job and recovery

**Proposed model.** A workspace-scoped capture job owns the occurrence, meeting, requester/consent receipts, provider/region, remote IDs, state, attempt, deadline, last heartbeat and stop reason. Store it in Postgres. Enqueue only its ID on a separate BullMQ capture queue after commit. Give each operation a deterministic job ID, a short lease and a generation counter so a stale worker cannot start or publish after a stop. A sweeper re-enqueues missing work and polls stale provider receipts. The job exists before any recording or processing run. The table follows the A/C participant flow. Historical B secured Hub consent before connecting, then waited for Google’s native approval. [Existing Postgres/BullMQ pattern](../adr/0004-processing-pipeline.md)

| State                              | Trigger and next action                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `requested`                        | Reserve the authorized occurrence and idempotency key in one transaction. A repeat click returns the same job. Cancellation or an expired request prevents dispatch.                                                         |
| `joining`                          | Claim capacity and dispatch once. Save the container/session/bot receipt. For C use `bot.joining_call`. On admission go to consent. On a knock go to `waiting_for_admit`.                                                    |
| `waiting_for_admit`                | Show the recorder’s exact name and remaining wait. A uses the Meet screen, B the session waiting state, C `bot.in_waiting_room`. A denied knock fails immediately.                                                           |
| `waiting_for_consent`              | Bot admitted, recorder off. For C expect `bot.in_call_not_recording`. Require the human consent receipt and a still-authorized job before issuing start. Show `recording` only after capture actually starts.                |
| `recording`                        | Track start time, liveness, deadline and stop request. Normal conference end or last-human grace leads to `finalizing`. Explicit stop, withdrawal or removal goes to cancellation and cleanup.                               |
| `finalizing`                       | Reconcile the end reason, finalize or fetch the consented audio, validate it and complete one original. An ambiguous end is held for confirmation until expiry. Empty, corrupt or unauthorized output fails or is discarded. |
| `processing`                       | The committed upload drives the existing run. Mirror its progress. Show **Document ready** only after v3 publishes the document. Analysis failure shows **Transcript ready, document failed** (`partially_ready`).           |
| `completed`, `failed`, `cancelled` | Persist the outcome and cleanup status. Show a redacted reason and recovery action. A terminal job never sends another bot.                                                                                                  |

**Proposed clocks, measured from each state.** Allow 2 minutes to dispatch, 5 minutes for admission, 2 minutes for consent and 60 seconds alone after a human has been present. Cap pilot capture at 60 minutes or the deployment limit, whichever is lower. On the cap, label any authorized result as shortened. At 15 minutes awaiting media, show delayed import. Stop trying at the earlier of vendor expiry or 24 hours after call end, even if the provider never reports ready. A capture watchdog operates independently of the UI. For C configure matching provider timeouts, including the no-one-joined and in-call limits. If the host never admits, withdraw once, verify the bot has left, release the slot, mark `failed / ADMISSION_TIMEOUT` and tell the requester exactly what happened. There are zero originals, upload events or runs in this case. Five billable minutes on a standard Recall bot ending in `done` would cost about $0.04. A new explicit request needs a valid admission window. [Recall configurable timers](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/automatic-leaving-behavior) · [Recall usage](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/calculating-usage) · [Bot status events](https://docs.recall.ai/docs/bot-status-change-events)

**Retries and cancellation.** Allow up to three dispatch attempts only for requests known to have been rejected before a bot was created. Retry transient reads/transfers with bounded exponential backoff and jitter. Honor `Retry-After` and the applicable deadline. A lost response to Create Bot or Start Recording is ambiguous. Direct Create Bot has no built-in deduplication. Save dispatch intent first, correlate signed callbacks and list/retrieve existing resources by job correlation and time before any second side effect. If existence stays uncertain, fail for reconciliation and keep the stop/cleanup watchdog active. Never automatically knock again after denial, kick, CAPTCHA or consent loss. Duplicate and out-of-order events cannot move a job backwards. On cancellation or meeting deletion, persist a tombstone first, stop/leave, abort multipart work and purge vendor/local copies. A late `done` may trigger cleanup only. A mid-call crash cannot restore missing speech. Label a consent-valid partial capture for organizer review, expire/discard it if unconfirmed, and never silently fill the gap. A failed import or analysis retries from durable media. ## Recommendation

Use **C for an opt-in organizer pilot**, conditional on acceptable vendor data terms and the two real-call passes above. Pay for a standard bot, copy out the mixed original, and let Meeting Hub produce the transcript and cited document. Revisit A if measured volume or data-residency requirements justify operating a browser fleet and Google’s terms review supports it. Retire B from production planning. Success for a supported room means the user approves capture once in the call and gets the document automatically when it ends. A host or domain decision to block the bot remains authoritative. ## Proposed Phase 8 scope

Ship one workspace flag off by default, approved organizer/occurrence registration, paste-and-join for guest-permitting Gmail and Workspace calls, a visible named bot with a notice and consent gate, stop/discard, one audio import, and the existing document-ready UI. Start with one concurrent call per pilot workspace. Show the exact reason and the existing authorized manual-upload option for unsupported rooms. Defer signed-in bot accounts, arbitrary third-party links, calendar scheduling, self-hosted capture, isolated speaker tracks and video. Widen enrollment only after measuring join/admit outcomes, missing or shortened audio, document readiness, deletion and cost per completed document on both host types.

## Open questions for the product owner

1. Is organizer-only, guest-permitting coverage sufficient for the first pilot, or are restricted tenant meetings with an invited signed-in bot a launch requirement?
2. Which regions and participant groups come first, who verifies host authority, and what evidence of each person’s agreement must we retain?
3. Is Recall an acceptable processor in the selected region, with a 24-hour recovery window and deletion after import? What retention do customers need in Meeting Hub and at the STT/analysis providers?
4. What join latency, hourly spend, expected peak concurrency and monthly capture volume would change the choice between Recall and our own fleet?
5. Should user stops and removals discard the entire recording as proposed? May a host approve a clearly labeled partial document after a disconnect, and is voice diarization sufficient for the first customer?
